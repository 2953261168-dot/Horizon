rss · Atlas Obscura 新奇世界 · Oct 28, 20:00

**「The Historical Significance of the Salle du Jeu de Paume」** The Salle du Jeu de Paume in Versailles, France, is a site steeped in history. Originally built for the French nobility to play Jeu de Paume, a precursor to modern tennis, this wooden room became the backdrop for a pivotal moment in the French Revolution. In 1789, during the economic crisis of the kingdom, the Third Estate delegates, led by Jean Sylvain Bailly, took refuge here after being shut out of their regular meeting hall. They declared themselves the National Assembly and swore an oath to establish a constitution, marking the official beginning of the French Revolution. Today, the Salle du Jeu de Paume houses a museum that educates visitors about the Revolution, featuring a statue of Bailly, busts of revolutionary figures, and an architectural model of the tennis court. This museum serves as a tangible connection to a key event in French history, offering insights into the social and political changes of the time.

**「Impact on Ordinary Life」** The Salle du Jeu de Paume in Versailles serves as a powerful reminder of the French Revolution&\#x27;s impact on political and social structures. For ordinary people, this site offers a tangible connection to the struggle for rights and representation that led to significant changes in governance. It prompts reflection on the importance of collective action and the fight for constitutional rights, inspiring contemporary discussions about democracy and the role of citizens in shaping their societies.

She co-founded the United Farm Workers union and played a crucial role in improving working conditions for these workers. Born in 1930 in New Mexico, Huerta moved to California as a child and later became an elementary school teacher.
