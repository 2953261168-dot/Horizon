This idea, known as Lamarckism, was influential in shaping early evolutionary thought. Despite being largely overshadowed by Charles Darwin&\#x27;s theory of natural selection, Lamarck&\#x27;s ideas continue to be relevant in modern biology, particularly in the study of how organisms interact with their environment and how these interactions can lead to evolutionary changes.

**「Impact on Modern Biology」** Jean-Baptiste Lamarck&\#x27;s ideas, often overshadowed by Charles Darwin&\#x27;s theory of evolution, have had a profound impact on modern biology. His concept that organisms can change and adapt in response to their environment has influenced how we understand the evolutionary process. This idea challenges the notion of living things as passive participants in evolution, suggesting that they actively shape their own destiny and, by extension, the world around them. This perspective encourages us to consider the role of behavior and environment in shaping the future of species, potentially leading to new approaches in conservation and ecological management.
